THE PROBLEM

The Lunar South Pole Operational Bottleneck

☀️ Grazing Solar Rays & Knife-Edge Shadows

The Moon's minuscule 1.5424° axial tilt keeps the Sun skimming the horizon in a 360° circle. Horizontal solar panels yield under 3% efficiency. Modest 200m crater rims cast 20-km shadows, plunging regions into cryogenic cold (<40 K) with instant 150°C thermal drops that freeze rover batteries.

📡 Libration Wobble & Periodic DTE Blackouts

Lunar optical libration (±6.68° draconic lat, ±7.90° anomalistic lon) causes Earth to nod and wag across the local horizon. Towering massifs like Shackleton and Malapert repeatedly obstruct direct line-of-sight, severing communications with NASA's Deep Space Network (DSN).

Existing desktop mission tools (SPICE kernels, heavy GIS suites) require complex command-line configurations, minutes of execution time, and specialized licenses, creating an operational bottleneck for rapid site evaluation.
